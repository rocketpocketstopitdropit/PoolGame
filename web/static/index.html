GAME

Circuit Pool: each player is secretly dealt three of the fifteen balls; the other nine are neutral. Hit any ball first. Sinking your own or a neutral ball keeps you shooting; sinking one of your opponent's counts for them and ends your turn. You see your own targets and how many your opponent has down, never which. First to sink all three wins.

Pocket Wars: all six pockets start open. Sink a ball in an open pocket to claim it (it's ringed in your colour); every ball scores its number. Your own pockets keep you shooting; a ball in your opponent's pocket scores for them and ends your turn, unless you banked it in off a rail, which steals the pocket. A foul spots the balls back. First to 61 points wins.

Cutthroat: three players, three groups — 1–5, 6–10 and 11–15. After the break the table is open: the first player to sink a ball picks any group, the next picks one of the other two, and the last player gets what's left. Keep your own balls up and sink everyone else's; any ball you sink keeps you shooting. A foul gives the next player ball in hand and each opponent one of their balls back. The last player with a ball on the table wins. Against the computer you play two CPUs, both at the level below; pass & play is for three people sharing the phone.

Bumper pool: a small table with a cup at each end and twelve bumpers in the way. No cue ball: you shoot your own five balls (red, or white) into the far cup, your marked (spotted) ball first. Both marked balls go at once to open. Your ball in your own cup, a ball off the table or a jump shot is a foul: your opponent drops two of theirs in. First to sink all five wins.

Octagon bumper: a big eight-sided bumper table with a cup on every side and ramps instead of cushions — 45°, 6″ high, with a hard corner at the bottom. A ball thumps into one, loses speed and rolls back down; hit it hard enough and it goes over the top and off the table. Two to four players (or two teams of two), five balls each: you shoot into the cup across from you, your marked ball first. Every marked ball goes at once to open, in any direction (no jump shots). Your ball in a cup that isn't yours, a ball off the table or a jump shot is a foul: everyone else (in teams, the other team) drops one in. First to sink all five wins; in teams, a player whose five are down shoots their partner's, and the team wins when all ten are down.

The Gauntlet: an 80″ square bumper table with cushions, a cup in the middle of each side and 68 bumpers. Nothing goes in from where the balls start in one stroke. In the middle is the Cage: two loop-de-loops crossing over a hanging dish. Your own curved ramp points straight at your cup: hit it right, jump the gap onto the dish and it flings the ball on toward the cup. Through a loop, a deflector bumper sends it at a cup. A cup only catches a ball that's not going too fast. Two to four players or 2 v 2, 6 to 8 balls each, the octagon's rules: your marked ball first, every marked ball goes at once to open, and a foul lets everyone else drop one in.

COMPUTER SKILL
CALL POCKET

Tap a pocket to call it. A ball in the wrong pocket is slop: your turn ends (the 8 in the wrong pocket loses; a 9 in the wrong pocket is respotted). Circuit, Pocket Wars and Cutthroat use Every shot only (in Pocket Wars a slopped ball is spotted and scores nothing).

AFTER A SCRATCH

Bar rules: after a scratch the next player places the cue ball behind the head string and must shoot out of the kitchen: a ball behind the line can't be hit until the cue ball has crossed it. Other fouls still give ball in hand anywhere.

FOUL VOICE

Plays when someone hits nothing, hits the wrong ball first, or gets no rail with nothing potted. Tap Record and say it (up to 3 seconds).

Turn your phone sideways to play. Rock, paper, scissors decides who breaks (in Cutthroat the break goes round). Drag on the table to aim, fine-tune with the wheel (slow drags are extra fine, fast swipes swing the cue a long way), touch the cue ball on the right to add spin, then pull the power bar down and let go to shoot. Tap a ball to look down the cue in 3D: drag up there to raise the cue for a jump shot. With ball in hand, drag the cue ball to place it. You can hold the power bar and aim with another finger.
